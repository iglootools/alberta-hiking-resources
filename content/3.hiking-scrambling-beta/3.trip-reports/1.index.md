## Trip Reports, Blogs, and GPS Traces

A number of individuals in the community share their spreadsheets, trip reports, YouTube
videos, GPS traces, and more. The by-objective pages above are built from these.

### Canadian Rockies

- [Bob Spirko](http://bobspirko.ca/): Hundreds of trips going back to the 1990s, each headed with its area and date. One of the two widest archives here.
- [Explor8ion](https://www.explor8ion.com/): Vern Dewit's archive, filed by region and sub-range, and the other of the two widest.
- [Sonny Bou](https://sonnybou.ca/scrambles/): A sortable summit table with elevation, location, and ascent date for every trip — the single largest index on this page.
- [Andrew Nugara](https://anugara.net/log.html): The author of the Nugara scrambling guidebooks, logging repeat ascents of the Castle, Crowsnest, and Waterton for two decades.
- [Adam Laycock](https://adamlaycock.ca/Routes): Route notes tagged against the Kane, Nugara, and *Don't Waste Your Time* lists, with the [lists themselves](#lists) alongside.
- [On-Top](https://www.on-top.ca/): Summits and hikes, each with elevation, gain, and a map pin.
- [Spectacular Mountains](https://www.spectacularmountains.com/): Strong on Kananaskis, Ya Ha Tinda, and the Ghost, plus trips much further afield.
- [Peaks and Streams](https://peaksandstreams.com/): Dave McMurray's reports, concentrated on the Castle, the Crowsnest, and Waterton.
- [Bill Kerr](https://www.billkerr.ca/): Scrambles, ski trips, and alpine climbs, sorted by activity.
- [Voyageur Tripper](https://tripreports.voyageurtripper.com/blog/): Detailed trip reports covering hikes and scrambles in the Canadian Rockies with route descriptions, photos, and conditions. Paid subscription required.
- [Steven's Peak Bagging Journey](https://stevensong.com/)
- Annie Ouellet
  - [Annie Steve Climbing The Rockies YouTube Trip Reports](https://www.youtube.com/@AnnieSteveClimbingTheRockies)
  - [Annie's Spreadsheet](https://onedrive.live.com/edit?id=54745E0F0206ED62!sfbbfb3e593c84980b9ff1f2d127cf827&resid=54745E0F0206ED62!sfbbfb3e593c84980b9ff1f2d127cf827&cid=54745e0f0206ed62&ithint=file%2Cxlsx&redeem=aHR0cHM6Ly8xZHJ2Lm1zL3gvYy81NDc0NWUwZjAyMDZlZDYyL0VlV3p2X3ZJazRCSnVmOGZMUko4LUNjQnBMa21Jd3hPVWFTNjhPQzg1VkpLbEE_ZT00Om1vQkJOcyZhdD05&migratedtospo=true&wdo=2)
  

### Glacier National Park, Montana

Just south of the border, with its own community and beta:
- [Glacier Mountaineering Society](https://www.glaciermountaineers.com/): A club dedicated to the mountain environment of Glacier National Park, Waterton Lakes, and the northern Rockies of Montana and Alberta — so it spans both sides of the border.
- [Climb Glacier National Park](https://www.climbglacier.com/): The site behind Blake Passmore's [guidebook series](/hiking-scrambling-beta/guidebooks#glacier-national-park-montana), with route write-ups and updates.
- [Peakbagger](https://www.peakbagger.com/) covers the Montana Glacier peaks too, not just the Alberta Rockies.

### Glacier National Park, BC (Rogers Pass)

Rogers Pass and the surrounding Selkirk Range sit in BC's Glacier National Park, just west of the Rockies, and have their own dedicated beta:
- [Steep Sheep](https://steepsheep.ca/)
- [Virtual Hiker](https://virtualhiker.wordpress.com/)
- [Golden Scrambles](http://goldenscrambles.ca/)

Sonny Bou and Bob Spirko both reach into Rogers Pass as well, and are listed above under the
Canadian Rockies.

### GPS traces

These are not tied to one region, and cover everything above:

- [Peakbagger](https://www.peakbagger.com/): the Alberta Rockies, but also Glacier National Park in Montana and much more.
- [Strava](http://strava.com/)

## Lists

[Adam Laycock](https://adamlaycock.ca/Lists/) publishes the four lists the community works
through most often, each as a table of objectives with its region, elevation, and the
guidebook's own difficulty or rating. They are his personal tick-lists, so the completion
column is his — but the lists themselves are the useful part, and they are the clearest
inventory of each book's contents anywhere online.

The three guidebooks behind them are described on the
[Guidebooks](/hiking-scrambling-beta/guidebooks) page.

- [11000ers of the Canadian Rockies](https://adamlaycock.ca/Lists/11000ers-of-the-Canadian-Rockies) — the **mountaineering** list: the 58 peaks over 11,000 feet, and the long game for most people who finish the scrambling lists.
- [Kane Scrambles](https://adamlaycock.ca/Lists/Kane-Scrambles) — the **scrambling** list from Alan Kane's *Scrambles in the Canadian Rockies*, 177 objectives graded easy to difficult.
- [Nugara Scrambles](https://adamlaycock.ca/Lists/Nugara-Scrambles) — the **scrambling** list from Andrew Nugara's *More Scrambles in the Canadian Rockies*, 117 objectives, and the usual next step after Kane.
- [Don't waste your time](https://adamlaycock.ca/Lists/Don%27t-waste-your-time) — the **hiking** list from *Don't Waste Your Time in the Canadian Rockies*, 144 trips carrying the book's own verdict, from Premiere down to Don't Do.

## Facebook Groups

In addition to serving as general-purpose inspirational groups, these groups can provide timely feedback in terms of trail conditions, closures, etc:
- [Hike Alberta](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1644376389112687) is probably the most popular Facebook group when it comes to looking for inspiration, pictures, and practical information.
  It is a bit of a monster in terms of volume of posts, and the approach is to give a little bit of everything for everyone.
- [Wild Canadian Rocky Mountain Hikers](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1907088892741238) is a popular group for advanced hikers and scramblers. The moderation team is trying their best to promote best practices and knowledge.
- [Scrambling in the Canadian Rockies](https://www.facebook.com/groups/2251181818/) is a popular group among [scramblers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrambling). The Scrambling in the Canadian Rockies group is for sharing trip reports and conditions, finding partners, and seeking advice related to scrambling in the Canadian Rockies.

## How the by-objective index is built

The region pages above are generated from the blogs' own archives by
`mise run build-trip-index`, not maintained by hand, so a correction belongs upstream at
the blog or in the script rather than in the page.

Objectives are matched on an exact normalised name, which is why the same peak spelled
"Albert, Mount" on one site and "Mount Albert" on another lands in one entry. Names are
never merged on similarity: Explor8ion alone publishes both Mount Kitchener and Mount
Michener, and both Bow Peak and Owl Peak, so anything closer than an exact match is
confirmed by a person before it is merged.

Coverage is not complete, and an objective missing here has not necessarily gone
unclimbed — only unindexed. Voyageur Tripper is the one source on this page left out, as it
sits behind a subscription.
