Working out where to go? Browse the
[trip reports by objective](/hiking-scrambling-beta/trip-reports/ghost-front-ranges) for this area, and
check the [forecast](/weather-trail-conditions/popular-locations/ghost-front-ranges) before you commit.

Mostly public land rather than park, so this is the one region where the usual answer is
random camping rather than a booked site. Access is by unpaved road, and the Ghost's are
rough enough that a low car is a real limitation.

**Public land camping**

See [how it works](/accommodation/how-booking-works#camping-on-alberta-public-land) for the
pass and the province-wide rules; what is specific to here:

- **[Ghost PLUZ](https://www.alberta.ca/ghost)** — the Ghost and the Waiparous share one zone.
  Random camping is permitted, but **along Stud Creek Road it is confined to designated camping
  nodes**, which run from the zone entrance for the next 16 km. The nodes are free and
  first-come, have an outhouse and a fire ring, and still need the pass; camping between them
  in that stretch is not allowed. There are also eight established campgrounds in Provincial
  Recreation Areas inside the zone, and the province would rather you used them.
- **[Dormer/Sheep](https://www.alberta.ca/dormer-sheep) and
  [Panther Corners](https://www.alberta.ca/panther-corners)** cover the country behind Ya Ha
  Tinda. Random camping is permitted in both, 100 m back from any lakeshore, with no permanent
  structures.

Check the
[Interactive Recreation Map](https://esrd.maps.arcgis.com/apps/webappviewer/index.html?id=e5651574de1342d7ae8277ef415864be)
for the node and zone boundaries, and
[public land closures](https://www.alberta.ca/public-land-closures) before driving out.

**Ghost Reservoir** — Alberta Parks

- **Reservable** — [Ghost Reservoir](https://www.albertaparks.ca/parks/kananaskis/ghost-reservoir-pra/information-facilities/camping/ghost-reservoir/), the nearest serviced campground, on the reservoir east of the Ghost River road.

**Ya Ha Tinda** — the Bighorn campground sits by the Red Deer River at the end of the Ya Ha
Tinda road from Sundre, on the boundary of Banff National Park. It is first-come,
first-served, and popular with horse parties; there is no online booking.

- **Hostels and huts** — none. Neither Hostelling International nor the Alpine Club of Canada has a property here, which fits a region where the usual bed is your own tent on public land.

For a serviced site with facilities, [Bow Valley Provincial Park](/accommodation/kananaskis-canmore)
at Seebe is about forty minutes south, and the nearest hostel is
[HI Kananaskis](https://hihostels.ca/en/destinations/hi-kananaskis) down Highway 40.
